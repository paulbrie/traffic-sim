Bistrița's peak-hour traffic inferred from TomTom, and one-way streets for the centre

TomTom records how fast traffic moves on each street, not how many cars there are. This report explains how the morning's and the evening's traffic were worked out from those speeds, and what the simulator found when the centre's streets were changed: two-way streets made one-way, one-way streets turned round or made two-way.

How the traffic was inferred. The plan was run in trafficsim's own simulator, and the number of cars coming in and where they head were adjusted, round after round, until the simulated streets slowed down where TomTom shows them slow. Checked on roads the fitting never saw, the inferred traffic predicts congestion better than a guess from lane counts (error 0.202 → 0.193 in the morning, 0.187 → 0.177 in the evening), but it explains only part of the street-by-street pattern.

What to change. Of the 33 streets in the centre, one change clearly helps: Str. Ecaterina Teodoroiu one-way, southbound, along its whole length. The centre's delay falls by 12% in the morning and by 11% in the evening, with 8% less traffic in the centre in the evening, at 5–6 seconds more a trip across the town. It brings more collisions and stalls at the street's ends in the simulation, so those junctions need a look before it is built.

1. What TomTom recorded

From 16 November, trafficsim saved TomTom's traffic flow for the plan every 5 minutes, 06:00–09:00 and 16:00–19:00 local time: 575 snapshots up to 24 November, over 692 roads, each counted one way (a “way”). For each way TomTom gives the speed driven, and that speed as a share of its free-flow speed, the speed at night with no traffic. A share of 1 means no slowdown; 0.5 means traffic moves at half its free speed.

On weekdays the town is slowest at 07:55 in the morning (53% of free flow) and at 16:15 in the evening (59%); weekends stay close to free flow. The worst hours, 07:30–08:30 and 16:00–17:00, are the two peaks studied. Recording starts at 16:00, so the evening peak may begin earlier.

For each way and peak, the figure used is the median, over the weekdays, of each day's mean share during the worst hour. Only ways TomTom covers for at least half their length, on at least 3 weekdays, count: 303 ways. Days agree closely, typically within ±0.06. The slowest are Str. Ioan Slavici and Str. Crinilor (about 0.2 of free flow in both peaks), Bd. Republicii heading south-west, and Calea Moldovei.

2. From speeds to traffic: how it was inferred

Speeds don't say how many cars there are, so a model has to turn traffic into speeds. Here that model is the simulator itself: the traffic was adjusted until the simulated slowdowns matched TomTom's.

  1. Free flow in the simulator. The plan was run with almost no traffic (6 runs of 40 minutes). Each way's mean speed then is its free-flow speed in the simulator, the counterpart of TomTom's night-time speed. 485 ways are driven, 269 of the 303 TomTom ones.
  2. A demand with few numbers: a gravity model. Each of the plan's 70 ways in sends P vehicles an hour, and each of its 68 ways out draws a share A: a trip from i to j is Pi · Aj / ΣA, never back out by the road it came in on. That is 138 numbers a peak instead of 4,700 trip-table cells, which a few hundred speeds could never pin down. It started from 60 vehicles an hour per lane of each way in, each way out weighted by its lanes.
  3. Simulate, compare, adjust, repeat. Each round, the peak's demand was run on 4 seeds (10 minutes to fill the town, 20 minutes measured). Every car's way in, way out and the ways it drove were recorded. For each TomTom way, the error is the simulated share of free flow minus TomTom's. Each way in's P and each way out's A was then moved by the mean error over the ways its cars drove (weighted by the cars, and by each way's length × TomTom's cover): a way too fast in the simulator gets more traffic from the places that feed it, a way too slow gets less. 14 rounds; the best is kept.
  4. Daytime friction. Traffic alone can't make the simulated town as slow as TomTom says: before it gets there, the simulation starts to gridlock (33 deadlocks a run at 6,600 vehicles an hour, still 0.21 too fast on average). TomTom's free flow is the night's, and by day parked cars, people crossing, buses and bikes slow town streets whatever the traffic; the simulator models none of them. So one network-wide factor φ scales the simulated shares, fitted between 0.75 and 1, and the demand explains the rest. It settled at its floor, φ = 0.75: by day a street is taken to run at 75% of its night-time speed before any traffic slows it further. Likewise, a round where the simulation gridlocks takes 15% off every way in, since Bistrița doesn't gridlock every day.
  5. Real or just fitted? Roads held out. The roads were split in two. The calibration ran on one half only and was scored on the other half, which it never saw.

3. Optimizing the centre: which streets to change

The centre is the area inside Bd. Republicii, Bd. Gen. Grigore Bălan, Str. Ecaterina Teodoroiu, Str. Parcului, Str. Dogarilor and Str. Alexandru Odobescu: Piața Centrală, Piața Mică, Piața Unirii, Str. Dornei, Str. Nicolae Titulescu and so on. It has no traffic lights.

The changes allowed:

  • a two-lane two-way street (one lane each way) made one-way on both lanes, in either direction;
  • a two-lane one-way street made one lane each way;
  • a one-lane one-way street turned round.

A change applies to a whole street, all its pieces of the same name joined end to end, since turning round half a one-way street would point the halves at each other. The candidates are the centre's 33 named streets, not the boulevards around it. Each change rebuilds the turns at both ends of the street the way one would draw them (straight on lane to lane, left from the leftmost lane, right from the rightmost, no U-turns), and is refused when it would cut any trip off, or leave any lane in the plan with no way onto it or off it. Most single changes in the centre are refused because they would cut a pocket of streets off: 11 are sound.

The score. Each design was run in both peaks, with today's layout on the same seeds. Its score is half the centre's delay (vehicle-hours lost an hour on its streets) and half the time per trip across the whole town, both against today: 1 is today, lower is better. A trip the town can't make counts as 10 minutes, so pushing traffic out of the centre can't win by stranding it. The search is greedy: each round it tries every sound change on top of those already kept, and keeps the best if it beats the current design by more than 0.01, about the runs' noise.

What it does. Str. Ecaterina Teodoroiu carries two lanes south, from Piața Unirii and Str. Liviu Rebreanu to Str. Zorilor and Str. Barbu Lăutaru. Northbound traffic takes the ring, Bd. Gen. Grigore Bălan, instead of crossing that corner. The centre carries 3%–8% fewer vehicle-kilometres, at a few seconds more a trip.

4. In the plan

The two demand matrices and the change were delivered to the plan as agent patches. The first, #7, made only one piece of Ecaterina Teodoroiu one-way and also turned Str. Liviu Rebreanu round. It was wrong: the street goes on north in three short pieces, joined outside any drawn junction, that weren't taken as part of it, and two lanes were left with no way onto or off them. The rule that refuses such changes for every lane in the plan, not only the lanes turned, was added then, and the search was run again; the results above are that second search's. Patch #9 corrects #7: Ecaterina Teodoroiu one-way along its whole length, Liviu Rebreanu back as it was, the matrices kept. Applied, it leaves the plan with the same ways in and out as before, no trip cut off, and exactly the layout simulated here.

The patches don't switch the demand in use: to see a peak, pick “Morning peak (TomTom)” or “Evening peak (TomTom)” in Demand, or “By the time of day” with the clock at 07:30 or 16:00.

Limits

  • TomTom gives speeds. Turning speeds into traffic needs a model, here the simulator plus a gravity demand, and a different demand that slows the same roads would fit as well. The held-out test shows this one is better than a guess, but only modestly.
  • The friction factor sits at its floor of 0.75, and the demand is capped where the simulation starts gridlocking. So the absolute numbers (about 5,300 vehicles an hour) are the simulator's, not a count. Real counts on a few roads would pin them down.
  • The evening peak may start before 16:00, when the recording begins.
  • The search changes one street at a time. Changes that only work together, such as a whole loop of one-way streets turned round, were not tried.
  • The simulation leaves out pedestrians, parking and buses (beyond the friction factor), and the collisions and stalls it reports are the simulator's, not a safety assessment.
