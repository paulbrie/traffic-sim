Can deep learning design a better town? A random city with Bistrița's traits

The goal: make the main road from the south-west to the north-east faster while taking traffic out of the centre. Only two main streets may have four lanes; every other street gets two lanes at most. Every design here was run in trafficsim's own V2 simulator, the same engine as the editor's, and it is bundled in this file.

The city

This is a random city (seed 1) with the traits of Bistrița Auto Search:

  • a dense old centre with blocks of about 100 m, inside a ring boulevard (shaded);
  • sparser blocks outside it, streets missing here and there, and dead ends (car parks and courtyards, where trips start and end);
  • a main road crossing it from the south-west to the north-east straight through the centre, a cross boulevard, and two longer bypasses round the centre.

What this design changes

    Its results

    This runs 25 minutes of traffic in your browser (5 to warm up, 20 counted). It takes a minute or two, and the result should match the recorded run exactly.

    How the searches went

    Both searches started from the same 40 random designs and got the same number of simulator runs: 10 rounds of 10 designs, each design run on two seeds and scored on their mean. The score is half the corridor's trip time plus half the centre's traffic, each relative to today's layout. It adds penalties where the rest of the town pays: trips lost, and trips more than 10% longer. Lower is better, and today's layout scores 1.

    How well did the neural networks predict?

    Before each round, an ensemble of five networks was trained on every run so far: 262 inputs (the genes one-hot, plus six sums over them), 64 and 32 ReLU units, and four outputs (corridor, centre, trips, trip time). Each network's outputs give a score, and the spread of those five scores is the uncertainty. They then rated about 8,000 candidate designs in a few seconds and picked the ten most promising: the lowest predicted score minus its uncertainty.     Cross-validation, here

    Train networks on four-fifths of every design simulated, predict the other fifth, five times over. This runs in a worker in your browser, about a minute.

    Run the experiment here

    This is the same loop as scripts/dl-city-run.ts, with the same city, simulator, networks and search, using your browser's cores. Each run simulates 5 minutes of warm-up plus the minutes counted. Keep the budget small: with 7 workers, one round of 4 + 4 designs on a 20-minute count takes about 3 minutes.
